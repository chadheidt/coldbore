How does Loadscope decide your best powder charge and seating depth?

No single number tells you a load is good — a tight group that wanders in velocity will cost you at distance, and a flat velocity string over a wide group won’t hold either. So Loadscope doesn’t pick your best powder charge or seating depth on one stat. It scores every charge and every seating depth you tested across four things that actually show up on target.

Those four: mean radius (the average distance of each shot from the group’s center, so one flier doesn’t wreck it — falls back to group size when only that’s available), velocity SD (shot-to-shot consistency), vertical (vertical dispersion — what costs you elevation at distance), and a light flat-spot signal (how little your average velocity moves across nearby charges).

Each metric is put on the same 0-to-1 scale (lower is better) so they’re comparable, then blended into one composite score. Lowest composite wins — Loadscope tags it 🥇 and writes the winning charge or CBTO to the top of the tab. Every candidate also earns a "Best in:" tag for any single metric it topped, even if it didn’t win overall.

The four aren’t weighted equally, and the mix isn’t a fixed table — Loadscope sets it from the distance you tested at. Up close, mean radius leads and velocity counts for less; at 1000 yards velocity SD takes over, because that’s where a few fps of spread opens real vertical. Seating-depth tests lean on mean radius, since seating tunes group shape, not speed. Every weight is still yours — the app’s Scoring tab lets you type a percentage against each metric, and Use recommended weights hands the dial back to Loadscope. Read the full method →

How accurate is the predicted DOPE?

Loadscope uses a G7/G1 point-mass ballistic solver — the same model class as Strelok and GeoBallistics — checked against JBM Ballistics (while it was online) and Applied Ballistics’ published range card. Measured-drag solvers like Applied Ballistics and Hornady 4DOF pull ahead deep in the transonic. Predicted values are an estimate, not a measurement — always confirm by live fire at known distances before relying on them for a shot that matters.

Predicted vs confirmed: gray italic numbers are the solver’s prediction. At the range you type what you actually dialed and that value turns from gray italic to solid text once confirmed. Predictions never overwrite confirmed data, and your printed DOPE Card carries a “verify at the range” banner until you confirm your first dial; after that, confirmed dials print in bold and the rest are the solver’s prediction.

Ballistic coefficient: the most popular match bullets have their BCs built in. For any other bullet, type its BC off the box or the maker’s site once.

Do I still need ShotView to get my Xero data in?

No. Plug the Xero into your Mac or Windows computer with its USB cable and Loadscope notices it, tells you how many sessions are on it, and offers to read them — every session at once, straight off the chronograph. Since a Xero holds up to 50 sessions, it asks which ones belong to this ladder and lists them by time, shot count, average speed and SD, grouped by range day, with the likely block already picked for you. It never writes to or erases anything on the chronograph. The Xero stores its sessions as Garmin .FIT files, and Loadscope reads those directly — drag one in on its own if you’ve already copied them off.

No cable with you? Export the session as a CSV from the ShotView app on your phone and drop it on Loadscope — it scores the same way.

Windows blocked or deleted my download — what now?

Three different things can happen, and each needs a different fix. Loadscope is signed by Loadscope LLC, so in every case you can check the publisher name before you allow anything.

1. Your browser paused it. Look at the top-right corner of your browser for a small download icon, click it, and choose Keep.

2. The file downloaded, then disappeared. That is Windows Security deleting it after it lands, which is why “Keep” does not help. It’s a false alarm on our installer — so download it again first, and it should stay put. If it still vanishes, open Windows Security → Protection history, find the Loadscope entry, and choose Allow on device. Check it says Loadscope LLC before you allow it.

3. It runs, but you get a blue “Windows protected your PC” box. Click More info, then Run anyway. The publisher reads “Loadscope LLC”. This is a one-time prompt that appears because our certificate is still new.

Still stuck? Email support@loadscope.app and we will sort it out.
