MagnetoSpeed vs Garmin Xero vs LabRadar: Which Chronograph?

All three measure muzzle velocity accurately — the real difference is how they mount and how easy they are to run. MagnetoSpeed attaches to your barrel: it’s the least expensive and works in any light, but it can shift your point of impact. The Garmin Xero and LabRadar are radar units that sit beside you and never touch the rifle — with the Xero winning decisively on size and setup speed.

This is a genuine three-way call, not a landslide, and the “best” one depends on your budget and how you like to work. Here’s the honest rundown of each, and who each one suits.

MagnetoSpeed — barrel-mounted, budget-friendly

The MagnetoSpeed is a bayonet-style bar that clamps to your barrel or muzzle device; magnetic sensors read each bullet as it passes. It’s the least expensive way to get lab-grade velocity, it works in any light, and it never misses a shot because the bullet passes right over the sensors. The catch is that it’s attached to the barrel: the added weight changes barrel harmonics, so groups fired with it on don’t always match groups fired with it off, and you can’t read velocity and shoot your real group at the same time. Best for the shooter who wants accurate numbers cheaply and doesn’t mind developing with it on, then confirming groups with it off.

Garmin Xero — the compact radar

The Garmin Xero is a pocket-sized Doppler radar that sits on a small tripod next to you. Because it’s radar, it never touches the rifle — no point-of-impact shift — and setup is nearly instant: set it beside the bench, aim it downrange, shoot. It reads every shot with per-shot velocity, works for essentially any firearm, and it’s small enough to live in a range bag. It costs more than a MagnetoSpeed, and radar can occasionally drop a shot, but for most handloaders it’s the easiest of the three to actually use. Best for the shooter who values fast setup and never wants the chronograph affecting the group.

LabRadar — the original radar

LabRadar brought Doppler radar to the range first and earned a strong reputation for accuracy. Like the Xero, it doesn’t touch the rifle and captures a lot of data per shot. The trade-offs are size and fuss: the classic unit is bulkier, its aiming and setup are more finicky, and it’s hungrier on batteries. A newer, smaller LabRadar answers some of that. Best for the shooter who already owns one and trusts it, or who wants the deep per-shot data and doesn’t mind the extra setup.

How to choose

  • Tightest budget: MagnetoSpeed — accurate velocity for the least money, if you can live with mounting it on the barrel.
  • Easiest to live with: Garmin Xero — smallest, fastest to set up, never touches the rifle.
  • Already invested / want max data: LabRadar — proven and data-rich, at the cost of size and setup.

Whichever you pick, the number the chronograph hands you is only raw material. The real work is turning a stack of velocities into a decision about which charge and seating depth to run — and that’s the same job no matter which unit you bought.
