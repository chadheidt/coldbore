Loadscope vs. QuickLOAD: Predict a Load, or Score the One You Shot?

QuickLOAD is the long-standing internal-ballistics simulator — it predicts pressure and velocity from physics before you ever pull the press handle. Loadscope works the other end: it reads the real chronograph and target data from the ladder you actually shot and names your best load. Different jobs entirely — and they pair well.

It's easy to line these two up as rivals because both live in the load-development world, but they answer different questions. QuickLOAD answers “what might this load do, and is it in a safe window?” Loadscope answers “of the loads I just fired, which one actually won?” Here's the honest comparison.

What QuickLOAD does well

QuickLOAD has been the reference internal-ballistics tool for decades, and for good reason. It models the burn inside your chamber from a deep database of powders, cartridges, and bullets, and estimates pressure, muzzle velocity, and barrel time for a given combination. You can compare powders on screen, see how a change in seating depth or barrel length shifts the numbers, and get a sensible, safe starting window before you spend components at the range. For understanding why a load behaves the way it does, it's a genuinely valuable tool.

Where it's limited

  • It's a prediction, not a measurement. QuickLOAD models an idealized barrel; your real barrel, brass, primers, and conditions will differ. Its own guidance is to treat the output as a starting point and cross-reference published data — the range still has the final say.
  • It doesn't read your real range data. QuickLOAD doesn't import your chronograph sessions or weigh what you actually shot; it works from the model, not your results.
  • No group measuring, no scoring. It won't measure your target groups or combine velocity spread, group size, and vertical to name a winner — that read is still yours.
  • Windows-only, and the interface shows its age. It's a paid, dated program (roughly $150), and Garmin Xero owners get no direct import of what they shot.

What Loadscope adds

  • It works from your real data. Reads your Garmin Xero over USB (and LabRadar, MagnetoSpeed, and more) — the actual sessions you fired, not a model of them.
  • Measures your target groups from a photo — group size, mean radius, point of impact — right in the app, no calipers.
  • Scores and names your best load. It weighs the velocity flat-spot, SD, mean radius, and vertical across every charge and seating depth you tested and calls the winner — from results, not predictions.
  • Predicts your DOPE and prints a pocket range card for the load it names, and carries it to your phone.

So which is right for you?

Honestly, this isn't an either-or for a lot of shooters. If you want to understand pressure, compare powders on screen, and pick a safe starting window before you shoot, QuickLOAD is a proven tool for that job. If you want a tool that reads the ladder you actually fired, measures your groups, and names your best load from real results — and prints the range card for it — that's Loadscope. Plenty of handloaders use QuickLOAD to narrow the window, then let Loadscope call the winner from what came off the target.
