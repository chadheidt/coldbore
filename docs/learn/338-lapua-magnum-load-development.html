.338 Lapua Magnum Load Development: A Practical Guide

The .338 Lapua Magnum is a serious extreme-long-range cartridge — it drives heavy, high-BC .338 bullets fast enough to stay supersonic well past 1,500 yards. It is expensive to feed and it recoils hard, which is exactly why you want a settled, well-developed load rather than burning barrel and brass chasing one.

Safety first: everything below is a starting point, not load data. Always begin at the minimum charge in a current reloading manual for your exact bullet and powder, work up in small steps, and watch for pressure signs. Your rifle, brass, and components are not the ones in someone else’s data.

Why the .338 Lapua is worth developing carefully

Its case capacity pushes 250–300-grain bullets with some of the best ballistic coefficients made, so a good load holds velocity and bucks wind at distances most cartridges cannot reach. But components are costly and recoil punishes sloppy shooting, so every round of your ladder counts. Careful charge and seating work here pays off in both performance and the barrel and brass you save.

What shooters reach for (starting points)

  • Powder: Retumbo, H1000, US869, Vihtavuori N570 and Reloder 33 are popular .338 Lapua powders; the big case wants slow-burning magnum powders — match them to your bullet weight.
  • Bullets: the 250–300-grain high-BC match bullets (Berger 300 OTM, Hornady 285 ELD-M, Sierra 300 MatchKing) are the extreme-range staples; the heaviest hold velocity best at distance.
  • Brass: Lapua and Peterson make excellent .338 Lapua brass; given the cost per round, uniform well-prepped brass is worth every minute.
  • Primers: large rifle magnum — Federal 215M, CCI 250 and similar are used to light the big charges consistently.

The process — same as any cartridge, .338 Lapua flavored

  1. Pick your components and look up the published min and max for that bullet/powder pair.
  2. Run a powder ladder. Load a few charges across the safe range and chronograph them to find the velocity flat-spot — the charge window where small powder changes barely move velocity. How to read a powder ladder →
  3. Run a seating-depth test at your chosen charge to tune how the bullet jumps to the lands and tighten the group. How to run a seating-depth test →
  4. Confirm at distance and record your dope, so the load proves itself where it counts — a cartridge built for extreme range has to prove itself there, so confirm your load and dope well past 1,000 yards.
