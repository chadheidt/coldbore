Loadscope vs. GUNR: Which One Actually Picks Your Load?

GUNR is a mobile shooting diary that keeps your rifles, load recipes, range sessions and chronograph data in your pocket; Loadscope is a desktop tool that takes the ladder you just shot and tells you which load won. GUNR is a genuinely good app — the two just overlap less than the feature lists suggest. Here's the honest comparison.

Both tools start from the same frustration — velocities scattered across a chronograph, a phone, and a notebook, with no easy way to see what they mean. GUNR solves the organizing problem across your whole shooting life. Loadscope solves the deciding problem for one ladder at a time.

What GUNR does well

GUNR is a suite, and breadth is its play: a firearms inventory, load recipes, range logging, and companion apps for ballistics and cost-per-round, all under one subscription. It imports from six chronograph brands — Garmin, LabRadar and MagnetoSpeed among them — runs both charge-weight and seating-length tests, plots multi-line velocity graphs with per-series ES and SD, and lets you attach target photos to a session. It lives on iOS and Android, so it is with you at the bench and in the truck.

If what you want is a well-organized record of everything you own and everything you have shot, on your phone, GUNR is a strong choice and there is not much point pretending otherwise.

Where it stops

  • It graphs; it does not decide. GUNR gives you an interactive velocity table and clean charts, then leaves the read to your eye. It does not weigh velocity consistency against what actually landed on paper and name a winner — and that judgment is the hard part of load development, not the plotting.
  • Target photos are stored, not measured. You get a photo gallery attached to the session. Turning those holes into a group size, a mean radius and a vertical number is still a separate job, with a separate tool or a set of calipers.
  • No target-app import. Because group metrics do not come in, they cannot feed a decision even in principle — mean radius and vertical spread are exactly the numbers that separate two loads with identical velocity.
  • Phone only. There is no desktop version. Comparing a dozen charges across three barrels on a 6-inch screen is a different experience than doing it at a desk.
  • It is a subscription. The bill arrives every year for as long as you use it.

So which is right for you?

If you want one place on your phone for every rifle, every recipe and every range trip, and you are comfortable reading your own velocity charts, GUNR is built for that and does it well. If the part you actually want handled is the decision — which charge, which seating depth, and how much to trust the answer — that is a different tool, and it is the one Loadscope is.

Plenty of shooters run both, and that is a perfectly sensible arrangement: log the season on the phone, develop the load at the desk.
