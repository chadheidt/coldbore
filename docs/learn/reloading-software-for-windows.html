Reloading Software for Windows: What Runs on a PC

Windows has plenty of reloading software, and much of it does one of two jobs: predict a load from published components, or keep a log of what you loaded. Here is what each kind is for, and where scoring the ladder you actually shot fits in.

The honest starting point: “reloading software for Windows” is really three different questions. Do you want to predict what a load should do before you fire it? Do you want to keep a record of what you loaded and shot? Or do you want to sit down after a range day, with your chronograph file and your targets, and work out which load to shoot? Each is a different tool.

Predict a load before you fire it

  • QuickLOAD — a Windows program that models a cartridge from its components. It is a computer simulation, so it works alongside published load manuals, not in place of them.
  • Gordon’s Reloading Tool — was a predictor that ran on Windows. The project removed the download on 27 July 2026.

Keep a record of what you loaded

Reloading logbooks and spreadsheets record your recipes, your round counts and what you shot. They are a good way to avoid losing a load you liked. A log tells you what you did; scoring a ladder and picking the winner is a separate job. If you keep a spreadsheet and wonder whether software is worth it, here is how they compare.

Score what you actually shot

This is the job the first two leave open. After you shoot a powder ladder or a seating-depth test, you have a chronograph file and a set of targets, and you have to decide which load to shoot. Doing that by eye off a spreadsheet is how people pick a charge from a lucky three-shot group.

So what should a Windows shooter run?

Most people use more than one. A predictor can help you choose where to start, a log keeps your records straight, and a scoring tool tells you which of the loads you actually shot is the one to keep.
